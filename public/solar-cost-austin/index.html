The Austin Energy solar rebate

Since 1 October 2026 Austin Energy pays a $4,000 rebate for a qualifying home solar system. You need an Austin Energy residential account, a new system of at least 3 kW (DC) that is mostly unshaded, and a participating solar contractor, who applies for you. The rebate confirmation letter has to arrive before installation starts. Step by step: how to get the Austin Energy solar rebate.

The current contractor list is on austinenergy.com. When we checked on 17 November 2026 it named 55 firms. 55 of the 69 companies in this directory are on it, and each profile says whether it is.

Value of Solar instead of net metering

Austin Energy does not use traditional net metering. It credits every kilowatt-hour your system produces at the Value of Solar rate, 12.89 cents per kWh for homes since 1 October 2026, and bills everything your home uses at normal rates. More in how the Value of Solar rate works.

Federal tax credit

The 30% federal tax credit for homeowner-owned residential systems ended on December 31, 2025, according to several installer and industry sources. With a lease, the company that owns the system can still claim a credit under Section 48E until the end of 2027, and some of that value may show up in the monthly price. Austin Energy does not allow third-party power purchase agreements (PPAs) in its service territory. Treat any 2026 quote that subtracts 30% for a purchased home system with caution, and confirm your situation with a tax professional.

Why quotes from Austin solar companies differ

  • Equipment: premium panels and microinverters cost more than standard ones.
  • Company type: national brands are reported to charge roughly 15 to 25% more than local installers.
  • Roof: steep, tiled or shaded roofs take more labour.
  • Hail: Central Texas sees several significant hail events a year, so ask about panel hail ratings.
  • Batteries: storage adds substantially to the price but provides backup during outages.
