Commercial solar companies in Austin, TX

47 installers in this directory take projects for businesses and nonprofits. Here is what Austin Energy pays for them, and who does the work.

In short

  • Austin Energy pays businesses and nonprofits either an upfront amount per watt or a credit per kWh for five years.
  • Both need an Austin Energy participating contractor, who files the application.
  • Owners of large roofs can also host solar under the Solar Standard Offer and sell the energy to Austin Energy.

Austin Energy incentives for businesses and nonprofits

Austin Energy raised its commercial incentives on 1 July 2026. A project needs an Austin Energy commercial account at the address where the system is installed, and one application per interconnection.

  • The contractor applies for you. Austin Energy only pays incentives for systems installed by a participating solar contractor.
  • Wait for approval. Projects installed before Austin Energy approves the application are not eligible.
  • No third-party PPAs. Austin Energy does not allow third-party power purchase agreements in its service territory.

Solar Standard Offer: solar on a commercial roof without using the power yourself

Austin Energy announced its Solar Standard Offer for commercial rooftops and parking lots in November 2024. The system is connected on the utility side of the meter and all of its energy is sold to Austin Energy for the Community Solar program. As the property owner you can own the system yourself or lease the space to a solar developer who owns it.

System sizePayment rate since October 2026
Under 1,000 kW-ac9.59 cents per kWh
Over 1,000 kW-ac6.92 cents per kWh

The program is for systems of 50 kW-ac and above. Austin Energy says projects installed after April 2026 are supported by a guaranteed minimum price for their first ten years, after which the rate follows the market-adjusted payment rate. The first project, an 812-panel warehouse roof installed by Axis Solar, was presented in December 2025.

Outside Austin Energy's territory

Businesses on the Oncor network, in PEC territory or in San Marcos follow those utilities' rules instead; the programs are listed in solar incentives by utility. The federal credit for business-owned systems is being phased out under the 2025 budget law, with deadlines tied to when construction starts and when the system goes into service, so confirm the current rule with a tax adviser before you count on it.

What to ask a commercial installer

  • Which projects of a similar size and roof type have you built around Austin, and can we speak to the owner?
  • Are you an Austin Energy participating contractor, and which incentive does this project qualify for?
  • Who does the structural review of the roof, and who handles interconnection?
  • What production do you estimate, and is any of it guaranteed in the contract?
  • Who monitors and maintains the system after it is switched on, and what does that cost per year?

Questions

What does Austin Energy pay businesses for solar?

Two programs. The Capacity-Based Incentive is an upfront check: $0.70 per watt for businesses with systems of 100 kW or less, up to $84,337, and $1.00 per watt for nonprofits, up to $240,964. The Performance-Based Incentive pays a bill credit for every kWh the system produces for five years: 10 cents under 400 kW, 8 cents from 400 to 999 kW and 6 cents from 1 MW.

Can a business in Austin sign a solar power purchase agreement?

No. Austin Energy does not allow third-party power purchase agreements in its service territory. A property owner can instead lease roof space to a solar developer under the Solar Standard Offer, where the energy is sold to Austin Energy.

What is the Austin Energy Solar Standard Offer?

A program for solar on commercial roofs and parking lots of 50 kW-ac and more. The property owner either owns the system or leases the space to a solar developer, and Austin Energy buys the energy for its Community Solar program. Since October 2026 it pays 9.59 cents per kWh for systems under 1,000 kW-ac and 6.92 cents for larger ones.

Does my installer have to be on Austin Energy's list for a commercial project?

Yes. Austin Energy requires a participating solar contractor for its commercial incentives, and the contractor submits the application for you.
