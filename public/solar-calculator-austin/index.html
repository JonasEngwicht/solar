How the calculator works

System size and production

The recommended size is your yearly use divided by the yearly output of one kW of panels, rounded to half a kW and kept between 3 and 20 kW. Covering about 100% of your use is the sensible ceiling with every Austin-area utility, because surplus credit is never paid out in cash.

One kW of panels is assumed to make 1,500 kWh a year on a south-facing, unshaded roof. Published estimates for Austin run from about 1,450 to 1,650 kWh. East or west roofs are counted at 85%, south-east and south-west at 96%, flat roofs at 90%; some shade removes 10% and heavy shade 25%. For your exact roof, NREL's free PVWatts calculator gives a month-by-month estimate.

Price

$2.84 per watt is SolarReviews' 2026 average for Austin, with $2.22 to $3.32 as the typical range. A battery is priced at $12,904, EnergySage's November 2026 average for a 13 kWh battery installed in Austin.

Savings by utility

  • Austin Energy: every kWh produced is credited at the Value of Solar rate of 12.89 cents, up to the size of your electric bill. The bill is worked out from Austin Energy's inside-city residential rates: the $17.35 customer charge, the four energy tiers and the per-kWh power supply, community benefit and regulatory charges. A 1,000 kWh month comes to about $140. The $4,000 rebate is included when the system is at least 3 kW, mostly unshaded and installed by a participating contractor.
  • Oncor areas: half the solar is assumed to be used in the home, saving 15 cents per kWh (the Texas residential average was 15.4 cents in 2025), and the rest is sold back at 9 cents. Buyback plans from retail providers range from nothing to about 15 cents, so enter yours. With a battery the home share rises to 85%.
  • PEC: solar used at home saves about 13 cents per kWh (PEC's March 2026 average bill was $162.31 for 1,250 kWh) and surplus earns the Sustainable Power Credit of 7.1921 cents.
  • SMTX Utilities: the rebate of $1 per watt, up to $2,500 and no more than half the installed cost, is included. Enter your own electricity and buyback rates; the defaults are placeholders.

Over 25 years

The value of each solar kWh is assumed to rise 2% a year, and panels lose 0.5% of their output a year. Payback is the point at which cumulative savings equal the net cost. Not included: loan interest, an inverter replacement, the property tax exemption, Power Partner event payments and any effect on your home's value.

Questions

How many solar panels do I need in Austin?

Divide your yearly electricity use in kWh by about 1,500 to get the system size in kW. A home using 1,000 kWh a month needs about 8 kW, or roughly 19 panels of 430 W, on an unshaded south-facing roof. East- or west-facing roofs need about 15% more panels.

How much electricity does a solar panel produce in Austin?

About 1,500 kWh per kW of panels per year on a south-facing roof without shade, so one 430 W panel makes roughly 645 kWh a year. Summer months produce about twice as much as December and January.

How long does solar take to pay for itself in Austin?

For an Austin Energy customer, the example 8 kW system costs about $18,720 after the $4,000 rebate and earns about $1,547 in Value of Solar credits in the first year, so it pays back in about 11.2 years. In PEC and Oncor areas payback is usually longer unless you get an Oncor incentive.

Can I still get the 30% federal solar tax credit in 2026?

No, not for a system you buy. The residential clean energy credit (Section 25D) does not apply to expenditures made after 31 December 2025. Leased systems are owned by a company, which may still claim a business credit and pass some of it on in the monthly price.

Does a battery pay off with Austin Energy?

Rarely on savings alone. Austin Energy credits every kWh your panels produce at the same Value of Solar rate whether you use it or not, so storing it does not raise the credit. A battery pays you through the $500 Power Partner Battery rebate and yearly event payments, and keeps power on during outages.
