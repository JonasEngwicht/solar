Is solar worth it in Austin?

For most Austin Energy homes with a sunny roof, yes: payback in about 11.2 years. Here is the arithmetic, and the cases where it does not work.

That is an Austin Energy home using 1,000 kWh a month, with a south-facing unshaded roof, paying cash at the 2026 Austin average price. On an east- or west-facing roof the same home needs 9.5 kW and pays back in about 13.4 years. Try your own numbers in the Austin solar calculator.

Smaller systems pay back a little sooner because the fixed $4,000 rebate covers a larger share of their price, while every extra kW earns the same credit per kWh. That only holds up to your own use: credit beyond your bill is never paid out.

What makes solar pay in Austin

  • Sun: about 1,500 kWh per kW of panels a year, above the US average.
  • A fixed credit: Austin Energy's Value of Solar rate rose to 12.89 cents in October 2026 from 9.91 cents. It is reviewed every three years, so it can move either way.
  • The $4,000 rebate, up from $2,500, via a participating contractor.
  • No property tax on the value solar adds, under Texas Tax Code section 11.27.

What works against it

  • No federal tax credit for systems bought after 2025, which used to take 30% off the price.
  • Cheap grid power: Austin Energy residential customers paid an average of 13.1 cents per kWh in 2025, below the Texas and US averages.
  • Shade, an old roof or a short stay. Replacing a roof later means paying to remove and refit the panels, so re-roof first if the roof is near the end of its life.
  • Hail. Central Texas sees several significant hail events a year. Check the panels' hail rating and that your homeowner's policy covers the system.
  • Loans: interest at today's rates can add years to payback. Compare the loan's total cost with paying cash.

Buy, finance or lease?

Buying with cash gives the shortest payback and keeps the rebate, the credits and the property tax exemption with you. A loan spreads the cost, but compare the total of all payments with the cash price, because dealer fees can be built into the loan. A lease needs no money upfront and the leasing company may pass on part of its business tax credit, but it runs 20 to 25 years and has to be taken over by a buyer if you sell. Power purchase agreements are not allowed in Austin Energy's territory.

Questions

Is solar worth it in Austin, Texas?

For most Austin Energy customers with an unshaded roof, yes. A 8 kW system for a home using 1,000 kWh a month costs about $18,720 after the $4,000 rebate, earns about $1,547 in its first year and pays back in about 11.2 years, leaving roughly $27,723 over 25 years.

Why is solar payback longer in Austin than in some other states?

Austin Energy's electricity is cheaper than the US average, and the federal tax credit ended for systems bought after 2025. The $4,000 rebate and the 12.89 cent Value of Solar credit make up for part of that.

Is it better to buy or lease solar in Austin?

Buying usually saves more over 25 years and keeps the Austin Energy rebate and credits with you. A lease needs no upfront money and may pass on part of a business tax credit, but it runs 20 to 25 years and has to be taken over by a buyer if you sell. Third-party power purchase agreements are not allowed in Austin Energy's territory.

When is solar not worth it?

When the roof is heavily shaded, needs replacing within a few years, or faces mostly north; when you expect to move soon and the buyer may not value the system; and in areas where surplus solar earns little and no incentive applies.
