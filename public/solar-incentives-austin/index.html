Solar incentives in Austin and Central Texas

What you can get depends on the utility named on your electric bill, not on your city. Here is every program that applies around Austin in 2026.

Which utility serves you

Austin has no retail choice, but its suburbs are split between several utilities. Look at the name on your electric bill:

  • Austin Energy serves the City of Austin and lists Sunset Valley, Manchaca, Pflugerville, Cedar Park, Bee Cave, Lakeway, Rollingwood, West Lake Hills, the Village of the Hills and Buda in its service area, though not every street in those places.
  • Oncor runs the wires in much of Round Rock and other areas where you pick a retail electricity provider.
  • Pedernales Electric Cooperative serves much of the Hill Country, western Travis County and Hays County, including parts of Leander, Cedar Park and the Lake Travis area.
  • SMTX Utilities serves the City of San Marcos, and Georgetown Utility Systems the City of Georgetown.

Austin Energy

Austin Energy pays a $4,000 rebate for a new home system of at least 3 kW (DC) that is mostly unshaded, installed by a participating contractor, who applies for you. Austin Energy's confirmation letter must arrive before installation. The rebate was $2,500 before 1 July 2026. Details: how to get the Austin Energy solar rebate.

Instead of net metering, Austin Energy credits every kWh your panels produce at the Value of Solar rate of 12.89 cents, up from 9.91 cents before October 2026. Credits reduce the electric part of your bill and roll over, but are never paid out. See the Value of Solar rate explained.

Battery owners can join Power Partner Battery: a $500 rebate for each battery that receives permission to operate after 18 February 2025, plus $75 per kW of average output during grid events each year. Solar is not required, and third-party-owned batteries are not eligible. More in solar batteries in Austin.

Sunset Valley homeowners: SolarReviews lists a city rebate of $1 per watt, up to $3,000, for homes that also receive Austin Energy's rebate. Confirm it with the City of Sunset Valley before you count on it.

Oncor areas, including much of Round Rock

Oncor runs a solar standard offer program that pays an incentive to registered installers, who pass it on in your price. The amount is set per project; Palmetto describes the 2026 residential incentive as up to $9,000, and Oncor's earlier rules capped residential projects at 15 kW (DC). Funds are limited each year and projects can be waitlisted, so ask your installer whether they are registered with Oncor and what your project qualifies for.

What you earn for surplus solar depends on your retail provider. SolarReviews lists buyback plans paying from nothing to about 15 cents per kWh, some of them only up to your monthly use. Compare the buyback terms as carefully as the energy rate.

Pedernales Electric Cooperative

PEC has no upfront solar rebate that we could find. Solar you use at home avoids PEC's full charges. Surplus you send to the grid earns the Sustainable Power Credit of 7.1921 cents per kWh since 1 March 2026, down from 8.2666 cents. The credit only offsets the base power charge, and unused credit expires at the end of each calendar year. Since March 2026 PEC also offers an interconnect time-of-use rate with a 12-month commitment.

Because exports earn less than the full rate, sizing a PEC system below your yearly use, or adding a battery, keeps more of the value. See the West Austin and Hill Country page for installers nearby.

San Marcos and Georgetown

SMTX Utilities pays $1 per watt for a new solar system, up to $2,500 and never more than half its installed cost, according to EnergySage. In Georgetown we found no current solar rebate from Georgetown Utility Systems; ask GUS about its interconnection terms before you get quotes.

Businesses and nonprofits

Commercial accounts follow different rules. Austin Energy pays businesses and nonprofits an upfront capacity-based incentive or a per-kWh performance-based incentive over five years, and its Solar Standard Offer pays for solar on commercial roofs and parking lots. The rates are in commercial solar in Austin.

Texas property tax exemption

Texas Tax Code section 11.27 exempts from property tax the value a solar energy device adds to your home, as long as it mainly produces energy for use on site. You claim it once with Form 50-123 at your county appraisal district (Travis, Williamson or Hays CAD), not with the IRS or your utility.

The federal solar tax credit in 2026

The 30% residential clean energy credit (Section 25D) does not apply to expenditures made after 31 December 2025, under the 2025 budget law (P.L. 119-21). It covered both solar and home batteries. Credit you could not use on your 2025 return can still be carried forward.

With a lease, the leasing company owns the system and may still claim a business credit under Section 48E for systems in service by the end of 2027, and some of that can show up as a lower monthly payment. Austin Energy does not allow third-party power purchase agreements in its territory. If a 2026 quote for a system you buy still subtracts 30%, ask the installer to explain it in writing and check with a tax professional.

Questions

What solar incentives are available in Austin in 2026?

Austin Energy customers get a $4,000 rebate through a participating contractor, Value of Solar credits of 12.89 cents for every kWh produced, and a $500 rebate for a new battery enrolled in Power Partner Battery. Every Texas homeowner can also claim the property tax exemption for the value solar adds to the home.

Is there a Texas state solar tax credit?

No. Texas has no state income tax and no state solar tax credit. The statewide incentive is the property tax exemption under Tax Code section 11.27, claimed with Form 50-123 at your county appraisal district.

Does Round Rock have a solar rebate?

It depends on the utility on your bill. Homes on the Oncor network can get Oncor's project-specific incentive through a registered installer while funds last. Homes served by Austin Energy or PEC follow those utilities' rules instead.

Can I still claim the federal solar tax credit?

Only if your system counted as an expenditure by 31 December 2025. For systems bought after that the homeowner credit (Section 25D) is gone. Unused credit from an earlier year can still be carried forward.
