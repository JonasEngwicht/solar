Solar batteries in Austin: cost, rebates and when they pay

A battery keeps the lights on when the grid goes down. Whether it saves money depends on your utility.

The federal tax credit no longer helps: like solar, home batteries bought after 2025 no longer qualify for the 30% credit. Prices rise with extra units, a main panel upgrade or a separate critical-loads panel.

Austin Energy Power Partner Battery

  • $500 rebate for each battery system that receives permission to operate after passing Austin Energy's final inspection on or after 18 February 2025.
  • $75 per kW per year, based on the average power your battery delivers during Power Partner events.
  • Solar panels are not required. Third-party-owned or operated batteries are not eligible.
  • Austin Energy typically does not call events when a severe storm is forecast, so the battery stays charged for backup.

The pilot launched in March 2026 and was expanded on 16 November 2026. Enrollment usually runs through your battery maker's app.

Does a battery pay off?

With Austin Energy, rarely on savings alone. Austin Energy credits every kWh your panels produce at the Value of Solar rate, whether you use it, store it or send it to the grid. Storing solar does not raise that credit. In our example home, adding a battery raises the net cost from $18,720 to $31,124 and stretches payback from 11.2 years to 17.7 years, before Power Partner payments. The reasons to buy one are backup power and those yearly payments.

With PEC or an Oncor-area retail plan, it can help. There, surplus solar earns less than the electricity you buy, so moving midday solar into the evening is worth the difference. In Oncor areas the utility incentive has often been tied to storage, which can cut the battery's cost. Run both cases in the solar calculator.

Backup: what a battery can run

One 13.5 kWh battery, such as the Tesla Powerwall 3, can keep a refrigerator, lights, internet, fans and some outlets going for a day or more, longer when panels recharge it. Central air conditioning or an electric range will drain it within hours. Many owners back up selected circuits; whole-home backup usually takes two or three batteries. Ask each installer for a load list showing what stays on.

Questions

How much does a solar battery cost in Austin?

EnergySage put the average at $12,904 for a 13 kWh battery installed in Austin in November 2026, or about $993 per kWh, with most quotes between $10,968 and $14,840. Whole-home backup usually needs two or more batteries.

Does Austin Energy pay you for a home battery?

Yes. Power Partner Battery pays a $500 rebate for each new battery that got permission to operate after 18 February 2025, plus $75 per kW of average output during grid events each year. You do not need solar panels to join.

Is a battery worth it with Austin Energy solar?

Not for bill savings alone. Austin Energy credits all solar production at one Value of Solar rate, so a battery does not increase that credit. The case for one is backup during outages plus the Power Partner payments.

How long will a battery run my house in an outage?

A 13.5 kWh battery such as the Tesla Powerwall 3 can run a refrigerator, lights, internet and some outlets for a day or more, especially with solar recharging it. Central air conditioning drains it in a few hours, so most owners back up selected circuits only.
